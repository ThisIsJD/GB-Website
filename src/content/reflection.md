**Looking Back, Moving Forward**

I enjoyed literature in high school, but Great Books helped bring that interest back. At first, I expected the course to involve a lot of reading. Ma’am Raquel made it enjoyable, and learning from her and my classmates helped me appreciate what stories can offer.

My biggest challenges were understanding unfamiliar cultural backgrounds and explaining deeper meanings instead of only summarizing events. Breaking the works into smaller parts helped. I also returned to important scenes and thought about why the characters acted as they did. This taught me to slow down and check my understanding.

**Reader-Response influenced my reading the most.** This approach connects a text to the reader’s thoughts and experiences. It helped me see why a story matters to my own life, especially when I connected The Odyssey to preparing for my career. The Psychoanalytic lens, which explores feelings and inner struggles, and the Historical lens, which connects a work to its society and beliefs, also helped me understand the characters and their world.

Across the seven works, I noticed how pride, fear, loyalty, and the need for care influence people. Different cultures express these struggles differently, but many of the feelings remain familiar.

As I prepare to begin my career, I want to carry these lessons with me. The Odyssey especially reminds me to stay patient, disciplined, and focused. I also want to manage my feelings and understand people before judging them. In my future work, being capable should go together with listening, continuing to learn, and considering how my choices affect others.

**Closing note:** There is more to learn, and I want to keep going.
