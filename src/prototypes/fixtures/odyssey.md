## 1. Literary lens and why

My main lens is **Archetypal**, which studies familiar story patterns, such as a hero facing trials before returning home. The **Psychoanalytic lens** also helps explain how feelings shape decisions. Odysseus’ pride, for example, makes him taunt the Cyclops despite his crew’s warnings (_Odyssey_, book 9).

## 2. Values emphasized

Perseverance, wisdom, and family loyalty guide the journey. Odysseus uses clever plans to survive, but returning to Ithaca remains his goal. His longing for home gives his struggles a purpose beyond fame.

## 3. Connection to its civilization

The poem reflects the importance of home, family, and hospitality in its Greek setting. Welcoming guests creates bonds between households, while mistreating visitors breaks an important social duty.

## 4. Connection to Bikol culture

I connect the journey to staying strong through hardship with family support. In Bikol families, close relationships can give people a reason to keep going, even when they are far from home.

## 5. Contemporary issue or SDG

The ending connects to **SDG 16: Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions**. After Odysseus kills the suitors, their relatives seek revenge. Athena stops further fighting and establishes peace. This raises a question that still matters today: how can justice prevent another cycle of violence? (_Odyssey_, book 24).

## 6. Personal reflection

I see the journey as a reminder that reaching a goal takes patience and the ability to change plans. Being capable does not mean every decision I make will be wise. Odysseus’ pride shows how wanting recognition can put other people at risk. As I work toward my own goals, I want to stay open to advice and remember why I started. Success would feel less meaningful without people I care about to share it with.

**Margin note:** Keep moving, but remember why you started.
