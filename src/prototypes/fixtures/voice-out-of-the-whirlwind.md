## 1. Literary lens and why

My main lens is **Formalist**, which looks at how a text’s words and structure create meaning. God’s repeated questions show the limits of human knowledge. For example, God asks where Job was when the earth’s foundations were laid (Job 38.4). I also use **Reader-Response**, which connects the text to my own thoughts and experiences.

## 2. Values emphasized

The passage emphasizes humility, wisdom, and faith. God’s questions invite Job to recognize how much lies beyond his understanding.

## 3. Connection to its civilization

The passage reflects an ancient Hebrew religious view of God as the creator and source of wisdom. It explores how people can face questions they cannot fully answer.

## 4. Connection to Bikol culture

I connect this to respecting elders and learning from their experience. Listening to others reminds us that we still have things to learn.

## 5. Contemporary issue or SDG

My connection is **SDG 4: Quality Education**, which supports learning throughout life. Humility and curiosity help us ask questions, listen, and continue learning.

## 6. Personal reflection

Accepting my limits gives me more reason to learn. I can be patient when answers are unclear while still trying to understand. This reminds me that admitting I do not know something is a useful starting point. Instead of rushing to an answer, I want to listen, ask better questions, and give myself time to learn from people with more experience.

**Margin note:** Some questions take time. There is still more for me to learn.
